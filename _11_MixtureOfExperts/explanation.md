## Mixture of Experts (MoE)

Mixture of Experts (MoE) is an architecture designed to increase a model's capacity without proportionally increasing computational cost. Instead of activating every parameter for every input, MoE uses a routing mechanism to select only a small subset of specialized **experts** to process each token. This allows the model to contain many more parameters while keeping inference efficient, enabling better scalability and specialization across tasks such as reasoning, coding, or translation.

The primary advantage of MoE is that it provides higher model capacity and improved performance with lower computation per inference compared to a dense model where all parameters are active. Experts can learn specialized knowledge, making the model more parameter-efficient and capable of handling diverse tasks. However, MoE also introduces additional complexity, including the need for a routing mechanism, load balancing between experts, and increased communication overhead in distributed training. As a result, while MoE offers significant efficiency and scalability benefits for large models, it is more challenging to implement and train than traditional dense architectures.
